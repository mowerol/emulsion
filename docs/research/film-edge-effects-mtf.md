# Film edge effects and per-layer MTF: what to port

Research for Oli, 27 November 2026. Not legal advice. It builds on [spektrafilm.md](spektrafilm.md) (October), [spektrafilm-2026-11.md](spektrafilm-2026-11.md) (24 November: the proposed **Film** stage) and [optics.md](optics.md) (the Halation and Glow port). It covers the two spatial film effects a per-pixel 3D LUT cannot carry: **development adjacency and DIR-coupler interimage effects**, and the **film's own MTF per colour layer**.

**Short answer.**

- **Both effects have one faithful source: spektrafilm's model, as darktable's spektrafilm module ports it** (`spektra_sim.c`, `spektra_core.c`, `spektrafilm.cl`). darktable is the porting rule's default, its C is validated against the Python, and it is the same code Emulsion's Halation and Glow already come from. Nothing else in open source models either effect for colour negative film. vkdt follows spektrafilm with its own shortcuts, spectral_film_lut's couplers are per-pixel only, and Filmulator models a different mechanism: developer exhaustion over millimetres, in black-and-white style.
- **Film MTF** is spektrafilm's **in-emulsion scatter**. It is stage 1 of the same `sf_halation()` whose stage 2 we already ported. It is a per-channel blur on linear exposure: a Gaussian core of 1.6–2.2 µm and an exponential tail of about 9–10 µm, with red the softest. It needs no Film stage and could join `stageOptics` as a small extension of the approved deviation (RGB instead of spectral exposure). It has to run at render resolution, not on the 2048 px optics fields.
- **DIR couplers** are spektrafilm's edge and interimage model. A 3×3 inhibitor matrix acts on density, and its correction field is blurred (a 20 µm Gaussian plus a 3 % exponential tail of 200 µm) and subtracted in log exposure before the film curve. This model needs the film's own density curves, so **it only exists inside the proposed Film stage**. It also splits that stage into two lookups with a blurred map pass between them. darktable's API is already split exactly there.
- **Datasheets support the picture but are thin.** Kodak's current Gold 200 sheet (E-7022, June 2023) has **no MTF graph**. Portra 400 (E-4050, 2025) does: red is the softest layer, and G and B rise above 100 % at about 10 c/mm. spektrafilm's scatter and coupler numbers are **generic for every colour negative stock**, not fitted to Gold 200.

## 1. Physics in brief

### Adjacency and DIR couplers

- **Classic adjacency effects** (Eberhard effect, Mackie lines, border and fringe effects) come from developer exhaustion. Fresh developer diffuses from a lightly exposed area into a dense one next to it, and exhausted developer and its by-products (bromide) diffuse the other way. Edges therefore overshoot on both sides. Wikipedia's summaries put the Eberhard effect at lines "approaching within 1mm" ([Eberhard effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eberhard_effect), [Mackie line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackie_line); secondary sources). The standard model treats the developed density as depending on the exposure at a point and on the density in a neighbourhood weighted by a **chemical spread function**, beside the optical spread function of light scatter. This is C. N. Nelson's model: "Prediction of densities in fine detail in photographic images", *Photographic Science and Engineering* 15 (1971), not read here, no DOI. A 1982 RIT thesis built on it describes it this way ([Cline, RIT](https://repository.rit.edu/theses/6469)). Earlier frequency-domain work: Lamberts measured sine-wave response ([JOSA 49, 425, 1959, doi:10.1364/JOSA.49.000425](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSA.49.000425)); Powell compared response "in the presence and the absence of diffusion effects during development" ([J. Photogr. Sci. 9, 312, 1961, doi:10.1080/00223638.1961.11736812](https://doi.org/10.1080/00223638.1961.11736812)); Nelson, Eisen and Higgins showed that one MTF is not enough for a nonlinear photographic system ([SPIE 13, 127, 1968, doi:10.1117/12.946758](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.946758)). **[measured/secondary]**
- **DIR couplers** (development-inhibitor-releasing) are the engineered version of this in colour negative film. Where dye forms, the coupler releases an inhibitor. The inhibitor diffuses through the same layer, which gives edge effects and acutance, and into the other layers, which gives **interimage effects**: purer, more saturated colours, and hue shifts near coloured edges. Interimage effects as a colour-reproduction term go back to Hanson and Horton ([JOSA 42, 663, 1952, doi:10.1364/JOSA.42.000663](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSA.42.000663)). For the chemistry, see Vittum ([J. SMPTE, 1962, doi:10.5594/J09317](https://doi.org/10.5594/J09317)) and Fujita, *Organic Chemistry of Photography*, ch. 13 "DIR Couplers and Related Compounds" ([doi:10.1007/978-3-662-09130-2_13](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-09130-2_13)). These were not read in full here. Kodak's own sheet lists "KODAK Proprietary DIR Couplers" with the benefit "Optimized sharpness • Distinct edges, fine detail" ([E-4050 p. 1](https://www.freestylephoto.com/static/pdf/product_pdf/kodak/e4050_Kodak_Portra_400.pdf)). spektrafilm's README says the same: couplers "inhibit the formation of density in nearby layers or in the same layer. This increases saturation and contrast. Also, if we let the couplers diffuse in space, they can increase local contrast and perceived sharpness" ([README L123-129](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/README.md#L123-L129)). **[measured/upstream]**
- **Datasheet MTFs include adjacency.** Kodak says so outright: MTFs are measured with "sinusoidal test patterns having an aerial image modulation of a nominal 60 percent", and "In most cases, the photographic modulation-transfer values are influenced by development-adjacency effects and are not equivalent to the true optical modulation-transfer curve of the emulsion layer" ([TI1301 p. 4](https://www.archives.gov/preservation/products/resources/5272-TI.pdf)). This is why datasheet curves rise above 100 % at low and middle frequencies. **[measured]**

### Film MTF per layer

- In a colour negative, the blue-sensitive layer is on top, then the yellow filter, the green layer, the red layer, the antihalation layer and the base. Fujifilm's C200 sheet draws exactly this order ([AF3-0249E §15](https://asset.fujifilm.com/www/dk/files/2019-09/cce1e1943550fc3e76c22411066f0100/films_c200_datasheet_01.pdf)). Light reaching the red layer has crossed the others and scattered on their grains, so the red record is the softest. Kodak's digitised curves agree (section 3). **[measured]**
- The optical part is a point spread function of light scattered in the gelatine and silver halide. spektrafilm models it as "an energy-preserving two-Gaussian mixture (core + tail)", "matching measured film MTFs" ([diffusion.py L76-L106](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/model/diffusion.py#L76-L106)). The measurements it was fitted to are in the author's "private halation notes" (same lines), not in the repository. **[upstream]**

### Length scales on the film and in pixels

All of spektrafilm's spatial parameters are in micrometres on the film. A pixel is `film_format_mm × 1000 / long edge` ([resize.py L22](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/runtime/services/resize.py#L22); darktable's `pixel_um`, [spektrafilm.c L2409](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/iop/spektrafilm.c#L2409)). Emulsion uses the same 36 mm frame (`FRAME_UM`, [optics.ts](../../src/engine/optics.ts)). The arithmetic below is **[inference]**:

| On a 36 mm frame, long edge | µm per px | Nyquist (c/mm) | DIR core σ 20 µm | DIR tail λ 200 µm | Scatter core σ 2.2 µm (R) | Scatter tail λ 9.3 µm (R); widest component 2.77 × 9.7 µm | Halation σ 65 µm (ours, already) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2048 (optics fields) | 17.6 | 28 | 1.1 px | 11 px | 0.13 px | 0.53 px; 1.5 px | 3.7 px |
| 2560 (preview) | 14.1 | 36 | 1.4 px | 14 px | 0.16 px | 0.66 px; 1.9 px | 4.6 px |
| 6000 (24 MP, 1:1) | 6.0 | 83 | **3.3 px** | 33 px | 0.37 px | 1.55 px; **4.5 px** | 10.8 px |
| 8256 (45 MP, 1:1) | 4.4 | 115 | 4.6 px | 46 px | 0.50 px | 2.1 px; 6.2 px | 15 px |
| 11648 (GFX 100, 1:1) | 3.1 | 162 | 6.5 px | 65 px | 0.71 px | 3.0 px; 8.7 px | 21 px |

Two consequences **[inference]**:

- Datasheet MTFs run to about 80 c/mm. That is the Nyquist frequency of a 24 MP image of a 35 mm frame. At 1:1 on a 24 MP RAW the film's whole MTF curve is visible, and the red layer is down to about 16–23 % at Nyquist (section 3). That softness is the "not pixel-sharp" quality. On the fitted preview the scatter is sub-pixel and invisible, which is also what a downscaled scan looks like.
- The DIR boost sits at 5–20 c/mm, that is 4–17 px per cycle at 6000 px. That is texture-scale local contrast, not pixel-level sharpening.
- Filmulator's developer diffusion is on a different scale: σ ≈ 2 mm over a whole development, a third of the frame's short side (section 2.5).

## 2. Implementations

### 2.1 spektrafilm (the reference)

GPL-3.0 code, CC BY-SA 4.0 profiles. The spatial stages run in this order. `expose`: highlight boost → camera diffusion filter → lens blur → **scatter** → halation, all on linear film exposure ([filming.py L39-L59](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/runtime/stages/filming.py#L39-L59)). Then `develop`: density from the curves → **DIR couplers** → grain ([develop.py L72-L113](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/model/develop.py#L72-L113)). Print follows, and the scanner's blur and unsharp mask come last ([scanning.py L124-L129](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/runtime/stages/scanning.py#L124-L129)). **[upstream]**

**In-emulsion scatter (film MTF).** `E1 = (1 − s)·E0 + s·[(1 − w)·G(σ_core)∗E0 + w·Exp(λ)∗E0]`, per channel. The defaults are `scatter_core_um = (2.2, 2.0, 1.6)`, `scatter_tail_um = (9.3, 9.7, 9.1)`, `scatter_tail_weight = (0.78, 0.65, 0.67)` (R, G, B), with `scatter_amount` 1 and `scatter_spatial_scale` 1 ([params_schema.py L109-L131](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_schema.py#L109-L131); code [diffusion.py L94-L124](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/model/diffusion.py#L94-L124)). The exponential PSF `exp(−r/λ)/(2πλ²)` is replaced by three Gaussians with amplitudes (0.1633, 0.6496, 0.1870) and σ/λ = (0.5360, 1.5236, 2.7684). These are "Placeholder fits, to be refined with a least-squares fit against measured film MTFs" ([fast_gaussian_filter.py L295-L341](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/utils/fast_gaussian_filter.py#L295-L341)). The values are identical on `main` and `dev`. Scatter is spatial, per channel and linear. It stays on in preview mode and is switched off for LUT bakes ([params_builder.py L85-L125](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_builder.py#L85-L125)). **[upstream]**

**DIR couplers** ([couplers.py](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/model/couplers.py)). This is the `dev` model, which darktable follows (2.2):

1. Density `D = curves_after(log E)` per channel, base removed.
2. Donor release: a Langmuir saturation `g(D) = D·(K + D_ref)/(K + D)`, with `K = k·d_max`, `D_ref = d_max/2` and `k = 1` ([L11-L47](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/model/couplers.py#L11-L47)). It replaces `main`'s linear donor and the older `high_exposure_shift`.
3. Correction `c_m = Σ_k g(D_k)·M[k][m]`, where rows are donors and columns receivers. `M = diag(gamma_samelayer)·inhibition_samelayer + off-diagonal interlayer gammas·inhibition_interlayer`, scaled by `amount` ([L143-L175](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/model/couplers.py#L143-L175)).
4. **Spatial step:** `c ← (1 − w)·G(σ)∗c + w·Exp(λ)∗c`, with σ = `diffusion_size_um` 20, λ = `diffusion_tail_um` 200, and w = `diffusion_tail_weight` 0.03 on `dev` (0.06 on `main`) ([L220-L222](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/model/couplers.py#L220-L222); [params_schema.py L135-L148](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_schema.py#L135-L148)). The tail's comment: "exponential tail for Lévy-like processes or environmental heterogeneity".
5. `D_out = curves_before(log E − c)`. Here `curves_before` is the datasheet curve un-inhibited, so that a uniform grey ramp reproduces the datasheet exactly ([L101-L140](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/model/couplers.py#L101-L140), [L231-L310](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/model/couplers.py#L231-L310)).
6. Reversal films instead get a linear donor (silver = d_max − D) and a Langmuir term on the receiver (Shuto et al. 1997, cited in the code).

The matrix for colour negatives is a class default. It was "Optimized on 2026/06/07 from study a22": `gamma_samelayer_rgb = [0.5159, 0.5934, 0.2829]`, `r_to_gb = [0.4032, 0.2488]`, `g_to_rb = [0.2227, 0.4340]`, `b_to_rg = [0.1829, 0.1799]` ([couplers.toml L14-L27](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/data/presets/couplers.toml#L14-L27)). Only four slide films and the two cine classes have their own values (same file). **Gold 200, Portra and every other colour negative still film use the class default.** The 20 µm and 200 µm sizes are schema defaults with no cited measurement. **[upstream]**

The old agx-emulsion names in the brief (`amount`, `ratio_rgb`, `diffusion_interlayer`, `diffusion_size_um`, `high_exposure_shift`) were replaced on 1 April 2026 ([`2f3327f`](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/commit/2f3327f) "couplers matrix as gammas"). Before that, the defaults were 10 µm diffusion and an interlayer matrix made by Gaussian-blurring the identity across the layer axis with σ = 2 ([params_schema.py L67-L73 at ec5b8fb](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/ec5b8fb2a76e2aa0fbbd16b1759cc1f0c8610186/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_schema.py#L67-L73)). **[upstream]**

**Single pass vs iteration.** `dev` computes the correction once, from the unblurred density. The unmerged branch `couplers-tuning-iterations` iterates `D_{k+1} = D_0(log E − G∗(M·D_k))` three times, because one pass under-corrects at edges ([`a273e49`](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/commit/a273e49b4243d19d7447df39c2b9880e9ec35a66), April 2026, on the old parameter set). It is not in `dev`, `experimental` or darktable. **[upstream]**

**Other blurs, all in pixels rather than µm.** These are the grain's `blur` 0.8 px (0.89 on `experimental`) and its multiplicative density USM (σ 0.7 px, amount 1.5; "make the pixels disappear", study b80, [diffusion.py L24-L58](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/model/diffusion.py#L24-L58)). The scanner's `lens_blur` defaults to 0 and its additive `unsharp_mask` to σ 0.7 px with amount 0.7 ([params_schema.py L81-L87](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/6cd00c8d4f30b5b550f50f4bbd3753c9f2a48507/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_schema.py#L81-L87)). Camera `lens_blur_um` defaults to 0 (L53). **[upstream]**

### 2.2 darktable's spektrafilm module

GPL-3.0-or-later, "Film modeling powered by spektrafilm … GPLv3" ([spektrafilm.c L19-L59](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/iop/spektrafilm.c#L19-L59)). **It carries both effects, with the same maths and defaults as spektrafilm `dev`**:

- **Split of per-pixel and spatial code.** `spektra_sim.c` is "all deterministic, all pure per-pixel"; "the stochastic / spatial effects (grain, halation, scatter, diffusion filters, coupler diffusion blur) are intentionally *not* in this engine … and stay in the caller". The stated split is `log_raw --develop_corr--> DIR coupler correction [caller: blur]` → `(log_raw, corr) --develop--> cmy film density` ([spektra_sim.h L25-L55](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/common/spektra_sim.h#L25-L55), [L680-L697](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/common/spektra_sim.h#L680-L697)). **This split is exactly what a GPU port needs** (section 4).
- **Couplers per pixel:** `sf_sim_develop_corr` (density from `curves_norm`, Langmuir on the donor, 3×3) and `sf_sim_develop` (Langmuir on the receiver after the blur, then `curves_before(log E − corr)`) ([spektra_sim.c L4829-L4895](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/common/spektra_sim.c#L4829-L4895)). Building the matrix uses the same gammas. B&W stocks use `gamma_samelayer[0]` on the diagonal only, with a long comment on why ([L4090-L4199](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/common/spektra_sim.c#L4090-L4199)).
- **Couplers' blur:** CPU at [spektrafilm.c L2469-L2512](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/iop/spektrafilm.c#L2469-L2512); GPU at [L3604-L3665](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/iop/spektrafilm.c#L3604-L3665). The blur is `(1 − w)·G(σ) + w·Σ a_g·G(r_g·λ)`, with the same three-Gaussian tail fit, shared as `SF_EXPTAIL_*` ([spektra_sim.h L426-L433](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/common/spektra_sim.h#L426-L433)).
- **User controls** ([spektrafilm.c L208-L229](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/iop/spektrafilm.c#L208-L229)): "DIR couplers" 0–1 (default 1); "inhibitor spread" 0–60 µm (default 20); "spread tail" 0–400 µm (200); "tail weight" 0–1 (0.03); same-layer and interlayer inhibition 0–3 (1). "enable halation and scatter" is one toggle, with "scatter amount" 0–1 (1) and "scatter size" 0.2–4 (1).
- **Scatter:** stage 1 of `sf_halation()`, per channel, exact or recursive Gaussians ([spektra_core.c L1082-L1160](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/c247126f9a7a2c3cdfc0f675a7698c24710438ca/src/common/spektra_core.c#L1082-L1160)). ### 2.7 Addendum, 27 November: a search for a paper-grade model like Newson et al.

Oli asked whether anything like the grain work's source exists for these effects: a published, physically based model with reference code (Newson et al., IPOL 2017). Short answer: **no**. The literature has the models (Nelson's chemical spread function, 1971; diffusion-kinetics simulations such as the 1999 Optica Applicata paper "Numerical investigation of sharpness" ([PDF](https://dbc.wroc.pl/Content/40473/PDF/optappl_2903p275.pdf), not read in full); Geigel and Musgrave's SIGGRAPH 1997 black-and-white development model, which covers resolution but, from its abstract, not adjacency ([project page](https://www.cs.rit.edu/~jmg/research/vdr/sig97images/))). None publishes reference code or colour negative parameters. Two more open-source implementations turned up:

- **Kallo** (tsukiyo20/kallotype at [`0f45c63`](https://github.com/tsukiyo20/kallotype/tree/0f45c639ee209dd88a0b806e7991d4cbe44cad5b), GPL-3.0-or-later, Rust + WGSL, created 6 November 2026, 3 stars, one author). **[upstream]**
  - MTF (`crates/kallo-core/src/mtf.rs`): one Gaussian per layer, **fitted per stock to the datasheet's MTF panel** over 5–60 c/mm. VISION3 500T: σ R 3.43 / G 3.67 / B 4.64 µm (blue softest). Portra 400 (`data/films/portra-400/REPORT.md`): σ R 4.45 / G 3.42 / B 3.11 µm (red softest, agreeing with section 3). **Gold 200: "borrowed", no MTF panel in the sheet**, the same finding as section 3. So which layer is softest varies by stock; "red softest" holds for Kodak's still colour negatives with a published curve, not for VISION3 500T. A single Gaussian has no above-100 % mid-band (the adjacency boost), which Kallo says it doesn't model in the blur.
  - DIR couplers (`couplers.rs`): saturating release per layer, a Gaussian diffusion in µm, layer i loses Σ M[i][j]·release_j of its density. Structurally the same as spektrafilm's. The matrix is "a placeholder ... colour negative film in general", like spektrafilm's generic values. A separate cyan-sensitive inhibitor layer model for Fuji's fourth layer (`interimage.rs`, placeholder strength).
- **Film-Juicer** (oldandangry/Film-Juicer, GPL-3.0, an OpenFX port of spektrafilm): carries spektrafilm's spatial DIR couplers. The same model as section 2.1, not an independent one.

Consequence **[inference]**: neither effect has a measured, validated model the way grain has. For couplers every implementation uses guessed strengths. For MTF the only measured data are the datasheet panels, which already include adjacency; Kallo's per-stock fits are the closest thing to "the measured numbers", but Gold 200 has none, and the porting rule's default stays darktable's generic core + tail.

What the data says **[measured]**:

- On every Kodak stock with per-layer data, **red is clearly the softest**: MTF50 is 34–40 c/mm for red against 53–74 for green and blue. Green has the biggest adjacency peak (1.17–1.23), as expected from DIR couplers, which Kodak advertises for sharpness.
- Fujifilm publishes one curve, so there is nothing per layer there.

**Turning curves into kernels.** A datasheet MTF is optical scatter times the chemical (adjacency) gain. It is exposure-dependent, and Kodak says it is "not equivalent to the true optical modulation-transfer curve" (section 1). It cannot be inverted into a single blur kernel, because a blur never exceeds 100 %. The faithful route is spektrafilm's split: an optical PSF per layer, plus the DIR feedback. For reference, a Gaussian PSF has MTF `exp(−2π²σ²f²)`, so `σ = 0.1874/f50`. A 2D exponential PSF has MTF `(1 + (2πλf)²)^(−3/2)`. Red's 35–40 c/mm corresponds to an "equivalent Gaussian" σ of about 5 µm, roughly 0.8–0.9 px at 6000 px **[inference]**.

**How spektrafilm's defaults compare with the datasheet [inference].** I linearised the model around mid-grey. The DIR part then becomes a gain `R(f) = (1 + a)/(1 + a·C(f))`, where `C(f)` is the coupler blur's MTF and `a = γ_before·g′·Σ_k M[k][c]`. I assumed a datasheet gamma of 0.65, and the Langmuir slope at `D_ref` gives g′ = 2/3. Multiplying by the scatter MTF:

### How the DIR effect differs from an unsharp mask [inference]

To first order, `(1 + a)/(1 + a·C)` ≈ `1 + a·(1 − C)`. That is exactly an unsharp mask with radius σ = 20 µm and amount a ≈ 0.7–1.0. So for small signals, the coupler edge effect *is* a USM. The differences that make it look like film rather than sharpening are these:

1. **Domain and position.** It acts in log exposure, before the characteristic curve, so its visible strength follows the local film gamma. It fades in the toe and shoulder, with no halos in blown highlights or crushed shadows. Our USMs act on scene light (Sharpen) or on print lightness (Sharpening).
2. **Density-dependent release.** The inhibitor follows the Langmuir-saturated density of the *donor* area, so a dense edge inhibits more than a thin one, and the effect saturates.
3. **Colour coupling.** The 3×3 off-diagonals make a saturated edge reduce the *other* layers' density across 20 µm. Next to a red object, the neighbour's cyan-forming layer is unaffected, but its magenta- and yellow-forming layers are inhibited. That gives local hue and saturation shifts at colour boundaries, not only luminance overshoot. In flat areas the same matrix gives the per-pixel interimage saturation, which is already baked into the calibrated curves for greys.
4. **Bounded feedback.** The gain saturates at 1 + a however fine the detail, while a USM grows with its amount. The 200 µm tail adds a faint, wide local-contrast term (3 %) that no single USM has.
5. **Physical size.** The radius is µm on the film, so it is the same fraction of the frame at every render size.

## 4. Fitting it into Emulsion

Today's order: Optics (Glow → Halation, scene light, fields capped at 2048 px) → Profile tone (sigmoid) → Look LUT (print, sRGB) → Clarity → Sharpening (RawTherapee USM) → Grain → Output → Scanner. The early **Sharpen** stage ("scanner sharpness", a log-domain USM) runs at the start of the scene chain ([ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md), stage table; [optics.glsl](../../src/engine/shaders/stages/optics.glsl)).

### Film MTF (scatter): near-term, no Film stage needed

- **Where:** in `stageOpticsScene`, between Glow and Halation, matching upstream's diffusion filter → scatter → halation order. It is linear-light, like Halation. It would be darktable `sf_halation` stage 1, ported from `spektra_core.c` like stage 2 (`optics.md` explicitly kept scatter off when Halation was ported).
- **Deviations to expect (questions for Oli):** (a) working RGB instead of spectral film exposure. This is the same deviation already approved for Halation and Glow. (b) Whether scatter applies to every photo or only with film looks or stocks, and its default. darktable ties it to "enable halation and scatter" with amount 1. CLAUDE.md puts Halation at 0, so is scatter "halation" for that rule? (c) Whether the Halation field should blur scattered light, as upstream does. The scatter is sub-pixel at the 2048 px field size, so the difference is tiny; computing Halation from the unscattered field would be a minor deviation.
- **Resolution:** it must run at **render resolution**, not on the 2048 px optics fields. At 2048 px it is ≤ 1.5 px and would be blurring the whole photo to 2048. This makes it a full-resolution map like Sharpening's `usm` passes. It needs four Gaussian components (core plus three tail terms) with per-channel σ, so eight separable passes with at most about 27 taps at 6000 px (widest σ 4.5 px) and at most 13 taps at preview size. No pyramid is needed: every σ is below the 3-texel level threshold of `componentPlan`, and below darktable's own 16 px decimation cut-off.
- **Cost sketch [inference, unmeasured]:** at the 2560 px preview, eight passes over about 4.4 MP with at most 13 taps is about 0.5 G texel reads. That should be 1–2 ms on Apple GPUs, but must be measured with `tools/browser-tests/optics.ts`-style timing. The field depends on everything before Optics, so it is remade on upstream slider drags, like the existing fields.
- **Interactions:** Capture sharpening (RawTherapee deconvolution of the *camera's* blur) runs before it, which is physically consistent: camera MTF removed, film MTF added. The early **Sharpen** (scanner sharpness) sits *before* the film in our chain, but a scanner sharpens the finished film image. With scatter added, an early Sharpen would be partly undone. Should it move after the film (question)? spektrafilm's own scanner default is an additive RGB USM, σ 0.7 px (pixel units, scaled by `preview_scale` in darktable), amount 0.7. RawTherapee Sharpening and Clarity stay user controls after the film.

### DIR couplers: only inside the Film stage

- **Why it needs the Film stage:** the correction is `M·g(D)` with D from the **film's density curves**, and it is subtracted in log exposure before `curves_before`. The neutral sigmoid has no density, curves or matrix, and the display-referred look LUTs (built-in looks and LUT packs) sit after it, so neither can host it. Approximating it there (a log-domain USM with a colour matrix of our choosing) would be inventing a model.
- **The split a GPU port needs** (darktable's own API, section 2.2):
  1. Film log exposure per pixel (the Film stage's exposure part).
  2. A **map pass** over the whole photo: `corr = M·Langmuir(curves_norm(log E))`. That is three 1D curve reads and a 3×3 per pixel.
  3. Blur `corr`: the 20 µm core (σ 3.3 px at 6000, 1.1 px at 2048) at render resolution; the 3 % tail (σ 107 / 305 / 554 µm) on the optics pyramid via `componentPlan`, as darktable decimates it.
  4. In the develop pass: `x = log E − blur(corr)` (receiver Langmuir only for reversal), then **develop, print and scan from `curves_before`**.

  Steps 1 and 4 can each be an offline 3D LUT baked by darktable's code: `sf_sim_develop` with an externally supplied `corr` is a pure per-pixel function of x. So option (a) of [spektrafilm-2026-11.md](spektrafilm-2026-11.md) becomes **two LUTs per stock**: exposure → log E (or a per-pixel port of it) and x → print. The single "couplers on, diffusion 0" LUT would remain the right bake only if spatial couplers are left out.
- **Texture units:** LUT B can reuse unit 11 for a film stock. LUT A and the blurred correction need units 14 and 15, the last free ones (13 is now the masks' Glow array, `MASK_GLOW_UNIT` in [params.ts](../../src/engine/params.ts); CLAUDE.md still lists 13–15 as free).
- **Cost sketch [inference]:** the map pass is a tap of the develop pass, like Sharpening's lightness tap, so it costs about a develop pass plus two to eight blur passes. It must be redone on every drag of a control upstream of the Film stage. That is the main risk against the 58 fps control target, and it would need the same tricks as Clarity: proportional scaling where exact, half resolution during drags. One shortcut is exact: the blur is linear, so the map can store the blurred donor `g(D)` per channel and leave `M` (and the coupler amount) as uniforms applied in the develop pass. A coupler-amount drag then needs no new map.
- **Grain:** spektrafilm adds grain after the couplers, on density. Emulsion's grain stays on print lightness after Sharpening, so neither scatter nor couplers would sharpen or blur grain. That matches upstream in effect.
- **Glow and Halation:** unchanged, and upstream of the couplers, as in spektrafilm (they act on exposure; couplers on density).

At 10–20 c/mm (17–8 px per cycle at 6000 px, the texture scale) scatter alone takes away a quarter to a half of the contrast, where the real film *gains* 7–17 %. In spektrafilm the scatter's softness is offset by the couplers' edge gain (section 3's linearised model). **So porting the scatter without the DIR couplers would make photos look soft, not film-like.** The two belong together, and the couplers need the Film stage. The prototype stopped at the reference for that reason. It wasn't wired into the engine.

### Deviations a port would likely need (for Oli to decide)

1. Scatter in working RGB rather than spectral film exposure (extends the approved Optics deviation).
2. Whether to port `dev`'s single-pass couplers (darktable) or wait for upstream's iterated version. Recommendation: darktable's single pass, as the porting rule says.
3. Splitting the Film stage's bake into two LUTs around the coupler field, if couplers are spatial.
4. Generic parameters for all colour negative stocks. Gold 200 has no MTF on its datasheet. Any per-stock retuning to Portra or Ektar datasheet curves would be our own fit, which is not allowed without approval.
5. Gold 200 on the 2023 sheet is coated on **0.10 mm ESTAR**, but spektrafilm tags it `still`/`weak` (triacetate, σ_h 65 µm). Halation stays at 0 by default, but if a Gold 200 Halation preset is ever made, that tag is upstream's, not the datasheet's. The same goes for Portra 400 (ESTAR Thick Base on E-4050).

## 5. Open questions and recommended next step

Open questions:

1. **Scatter scope:** every photo, film stocks only, or a control? Default on (darktable) or 0 (our Halation rule)?
2. **Early Sharpen position:** move scanner sharpness after the film's MTF, or leave it?
3. **Couplers:** wait for the Film stage. When it comes, should it be spatial from the start (two LUTs plus a map pass), or per-pixel first (one LUT with diffusion 0) and spatial later?
4. **Upstream drift:** `dev`'s coupler matrix and Langmuir arrived in June. `experimental` changes every profile but not the couplers. Pin darktable 5.8's code and pack, as recommended in [spektrafilm-2026-11.md](spektrafilm-2026-11.md).
5. **Validation:** what is the target for "looks like a Gold 200 scan" when Gold 200 has no published MTF? darktable's own render of the same RAW (exact port), or scans (which bring in scanner MTF)?

**Recommended next step:** prototype the **scatter stage alone**: a TypeScript reference from darktable `sf_halation` stage 1 with tests, a GLSL twin at render resolution, and the probe. It is small, it is the same code family as our Halation, and it can be judged at 1:1 on a 24 MP RAW, where the red layer's softness and the green/blue difference should be visible. Measure its cost with the probe and `node tools/perf/inapp.mjs`. Bring the deviations above to Oli first. Plan the DIR couplers as part of the Film stage work, with darktable's `develop_corr`/`develop` split as the seam.
