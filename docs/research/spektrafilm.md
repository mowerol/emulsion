# spektrafilm as a contained film-stock stage

> **Update:** darktable master has its own native port of spektrafilm (`src/iop/spektrafilm.c`, merged 2026-09-05, unreleased as of darktable 5.6.2), validated against the Python to within 1e-4. Under the porting rule, it should be the source for a bake or a port, ahead of the Python and vkdt. See section 8 of [film-stock-options.md](film-stock-options.md).

Research for Oli, October 2026. Question: can spektrafilm (Andrea Volpato's spectral negative, print and scan simulation, formerly agx-emulsion) replace Emulsion's hand-tuned stock numbers as one contained stage, without changing the rest of the pipeline?

**Short answer: yes, as a per-stock 3D LUT baked offline by spektrafilm's own code in its LUT mode, placed where Emulsion's Emulsion, Tone and Print steps sit for film stocks.** spektrafilm already has a switch that turns its pipeline into a deterministic per-pixel transform for exactly this purpose, and its author ships a LUT creator and endorses LUT bridges (ART uses one). What a LUT loses is the spatial and random parts (halation, scatter, grain, DIR-coupler diffusion, glare, scanner sharpening), and Emulsion already has its own halation and grain (both off by default). A per-pixel GLSL port of the colour path, following vkdt, is the second option: more live controls, much more work, and no more faithful for what Emulsion exposes today. The full spatial pipeline is not contained: it replaces our halation, grain and sharpening.

## 1. Inputs and outputs

### spektrafilm

- **Input: linear RGB in named primaries, scene-referred.** The runtime is "linear-in / linear-out" ([README.md L135-136](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/README.md#L135-L136); the LUT creator's boundary is stated in [spektrafilm_lut_creator/README.md L51-67](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm_lut_creator/README.md#L51-L67)). The default input space is linear ProPhoto RGB ([params_schema.py L170-171](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_schema.py#L170-L171)), but any colour-science space name works, including `ITU-R BT.2020`. The author's own workflow is darktable with filmic/sigmoid off, exported as linear float ([README.md L406-411](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/README.md#L406-L411)).
- **White:** the input is converted to XYZ with CAT16 adaptation from the space's white to the film's reference illuminant (D55 for still films, tungsten for Vision3 T) ([spectral_upsampling.py L119-148](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/utils/spectral_upsampling.py#L119-L148)). So a white-balanced D65 input stays neutral on the film: white balance belongs upstream, which is where Emulsion already does it.
- **Exposure normalisation: middle grey 0.184.** The film's raw exposure is normalised so a 0.184 grey gives a raw value of 1, i.e. log exposure 0 ([spectral_upsampling.py L327-328](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/utils/spectral_upsampling.py#L327-L328), [L404](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/utils/spectral_upsampling.py#L404)); the print is balanced on the same 0.184 grey ([filming.py L125-134](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/stages/filming.py#L125-L134)). **Auto exposure is on by default** and rescales the image so its centre-weighted mean is 0.184 ([params_schema.py L51-52](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_schema.py#L51-L52), [filming.py L40-50](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/stages/filming.py#L40-L50), [autoexposure.py](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/utils/autoexposure.py)); LUT mode turns it off.
- **Pipeline:** preprocess, film expose, film develop, print expose, print develop, scan ([pipeline.py L174-189](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/pipeline.py#L174-L189)), with named taps between them (`rgb_in`, `log_e_film`, `cmy_film`, `log_e_print`, `cmy_print`, `rgb_out`; [topology.py L12-25](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/topology.py#L12-L25)). `scan_film` skips the print and scans the negative.
- **Output: display-referred RGB of the print as seen under its viewing illuminant (D50), adapted to the output space and sRGB-encoded by default.** The scan integrates the print's dye densities against the viewing illuminant and the CIE 1931 observer, normalises by the illuminant's Y (so paper white is close to 1), converts XYZ to the output space with adaptation, applies output gamut compression that keeps everything in [0, 1], then the output CCTF ([scanning.py L46-49](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/stages/scanning.py#L46-L49), [L53-98](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/stages/scanning.py#L53-L98), [L130-139](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/stages/scanning.py#L130-L139); defaults `output_color_space="sRGB"`, `output_cctf_encoding=True` at [params_schema.py L172-173](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_schema.py#L172-L173)). Setting the output to linear BT.2020 is a parameter.

### vkdt filmsim

- **Input: scene-referred linear Rec.2020 "after the colour module"** ([readme.md L166](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/readme.md#L166)), i.e. after vkdt's white balance and exposure. Alternatively camera RGB with a camera-specific spectral table ([readme.md L225](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/readme.md#L225)). Each pixel goes Rec.2020 to XYZ to spectral coefficients ([exposure.glsl L12-19](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/exposure.glsl#L12-L19)); values are clamped to at least 5e-4 per channel first.
- **Exposure normalisation: middle grey 0.18,** fixed, not measured from the image. The "autoexp" norms are the response to a 0.18 reflector computed once per frame from the spectra ([setup.glsl L270-281](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/setup.glsl#L270-L281), [L337-339](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/setup.glsl#L337-L339)). The film log exposure is clamped to ±4 (log10) ([expose.comp L17-20](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/expose.comp#L17-L20)).
- **Output: linear Rec.2020,** the print (or negative) scanned to XYZ and adapted with CAT16 from the scan illuminant to D65 ([scan.glsl L9-21](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/scan.glsl#L9-L21)). No output gamut compression and no transfer curve; vkdt's display stage does that.

### What this means for Emulsion

Emulsion works in scene-linear Rec.2020 D65 ([ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md), "Colour"), which is vkdt's input exactly and one of spektrafilm's accepted inputs. The Tone stage's output is sRGB-encoded Rec.2020 ([tone.glsl](../../src/engine/shaders/stages/tone.glsl) header), so a film stage that outputs linear Rec.2020 (vkdt's convention, or spektrafilm with `output_color_space="ITU-R BT.2020"` and no CCTF) followed by the sRGB transfer function produces exactly the value Clarity and everything after it expect.

One thing to verify before any work: that Emulsion's scene-linear middle grey sits near 0.18. RAWs are scaled by `BASELINE_GAIN` 1.8 to match the camera JPEG's brightness ([ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md), "RAW files"), and ordinary photos are expanded by the inverse of the neutral sigmoid. If grey lands elsewhere, every spektrafilm stock will look over- or under-exposed until a fixed offset is applied in front of the stage. The probe can measure this.

## 3. Feeding our later stages, and what the sliders mean

### Where it sits

Today's develop pass ([develop.frag.glsl](../../src/engine/shaders/develop.frag.glsl)) runs: Sharpen, Exposure, Tone equalizer, Texture, Colour, Colour balance, Lens, Optics (halation), **Emulsion**, **Tone**, Clarity, **Print** (stock saturation and black lift), Sharpening, Grain, Output, Scanner, Curve, Finish.

For a spektrafilm stock, one new stage replaces Emulsion, Tone and Print:

```
... Colour balance ─ Lens ─ Optics ─ [Film: spektrafilm, scene-linear Rec.2020 → sRGB-encoded Rec.2020 print] ─ Clarity ─ Sharpening ─ Grain ─ Output ─ Scanner ─ Curve ─ Finish
```

Everything downstream keeps its domain. Clarity, Sharpening and Grain work on CIE lightness of the "print", which is what spektrafilm's output is; Output converts Rec.2020 to sRGB; Scanner, Curve and Finish act on the display image. The stock's balance gains in Exposure become identity (spektrafilm balances in the enlarger). Neutral and the original (Compare) never touch the new stage and keep the sigmoid and its exact inverse, so the input-to-original round trip is untouched.

Two details. Clarity's map and Sharpening's map are made by running the develop pass "to the print's lightness" ([ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md), "Passes"); those taps would read after the Film stage instead of after Tone, which is a one-line change in the same shader. And Emulsion's black-and-white path (`uBW`, `bwWeights`) stays for any B&W stock that is not moved to spektrafilm.

### The sliders afterwards

- **Neutral:** unchanged, darktable sigmoid.
- **Exposure:** unchanged; it is the negative's exposure (spektrafilm's `exposure_compensation_ev` with no print compensation). Over-exposure gives a denser negative and, with a fixed printer, a brighter print, which is what film does.
- **Contrast, Saturation, Vibrance, Shadows:** these are darktable colour balance rgb in scene light ([params.ts](../../src/engine/params.ts), `colorBalanceParams`), upstream of the film. They keep working unchanged and become "what the scene looked like" adjustments that the film then renders. That is a change of feel, not of code. spektrafilm's own contrast controls are film gamma and paper gamma ([params_schema.py L153](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_schema.py#L153), [morph_curves.py L65-75](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/utils/morph_curves.py#L65-L75)); vkdt's are `g film` and `g paper`. Its saturation control is the DIR-coupler amount (`cp amt` in vkdt). In a LUT, those are fixed at bake time.
- **Whites / Blacks:** Whites sets the sigmoid's display white and Blacks above zero its display black ([tone.glsl](../../src/engine/shaders/stages/tone.glsl), `toneLookFor` in [params.ts](../../src/engine/params.ts)); Blacks below zero is a scene black level in Exposure and keeps working. For a spektrafilm stock the print's white is the paper base and its black the paper's D-max, so there is no sigmoid target to move. The closest upstream controls are print exposure (`print_exposure` / `ev paper`, which moves the whole print density) and preflash (lowers contrast and lifts blacks). Neither is "Whites". Options: re-bake a few print-exposure steps, or apply Whites and positive Blacks as display levels after the stage (our own maths, so a deviation that needs Oli's agreement), or hide them for spektrafilm stocks.
- **Push / pull:** Emulsion's push scales sigmoid contrast by 1 + 0.09 per stop and saturation by 1 + 0.04 per stop, and doubles grain speed per stop ([params.ts](../../src/engine/params.ts), `toneLookFor`, `uSat`, `grainUniform`). spektrafilm has measured push data only for Portra 800. A faithful mapping for other stocks does not exist upstream; a push could be expressed as film gamma (`density_curve_gamma`) with its value fitted from the Portra 800 push1/push2 curves, baked as one LUT per push value (four values: -1, 0, 1, 2). That fit is our invention and needs Oli's agreement. The grain-speed part of push is ours and keeps working.
- **Halation, Glow, Grain, Vignette, Clarity, Texture, Sharpening, Highlights, Colour, Curve, Fade:** unchanged.

## 4. How vkdt is fast, and whether a baked 3D LUT is faithful

### What vkdt bakes

vkdt does **not** bake a colour LUT. `mklut-profiles.py` packs each stock's spectral data into one texture, three 256-wide RGBA rows per stock: log spectral sensitivity, dye densities with base density, and a row of model parameters (the three sub-layer centres, amplitudes and inverse sigmas per channel; for films also maximum density, DIR-coupler matrix, grain particle areas, uniformity, halation strength, minimum density, reference illuminant and an exposure normalisation) ([mklut-profiles.py L413-503](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/mklut-profiles.py#L413-L503); described in [readme.md L153-159](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/readme.md#L153-L159)). Coupler and grain constants are resolved from spektrafilm's TOML presets at bake time ([mklut-profiles.py L209-279](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/mklut-profiles.py#L209-L279)). The spectral upsampling table (`spectra-em.lut`, Rec.2020/XYZ chromaticity to three sigmoid-spectrum coefficients) is a separate input ([readme.md L169](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/readme.md#L169)).

Per frame, one small `setup` dispatch folds everything that does not depend on the pixel into per-band factors: illuminants, enlarger lamp through the dichroic filters, paper sensitivity, scan illuminant times CMFs, and the 0.18 normalisations ([setup.glsl L270-400](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/setup.glsl#L270-L400)). A `dirlut` dispatch precomputes the inverse DIR-coupler warp of the density curves on a 1D grid ([dirlut.comp](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/dirlut.comp)).

Per pixel it still does the spectral work: fetch the upsampling coefficients, integrate the spectrum against 36 bands for the film exposure, evaluate the density model, then 41-band integrals for enlarger exposure and scan ([head.glsl L83-86](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/head.glsl#L83-L86), [filmsim.glsl L20-60](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/filmsim.glsl#L20), [print.glsl](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/print.glsl), [scan.glsl](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/scan.glsl)). When couplers and halation are off it fuses everything into one dispatch ([fast.comp](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/fast.comp), selected in [main.c L142](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/main.c#L142)); otherwise it runs expose, coupler blur, develop and halation passes. So vkdt is fast because it is GPU code with precomputed per-frame constants and vectorised band loops, not because of a LUT.

### A baked 3D LUT per stock

spektrafilm supports this directly:

- `debug.lut_mode` makes the pipeline "a deterministic per-pixel transform suitable for LUT sampling" ([params_schema.py L197-205](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_schema.py#L197-L205)). It turns off spatial effects, random effects, auto exposure, exposure compensation, print exposure compensation, highlight boost (which normalises by the image maximum), and scanner corrections ([params_builder.py L99-142](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_builder.py#L99-L142)).
- DIR couplers **keep their colour effect** in LUT mode: only their spatial diffusion is dropped; "the non-spatial DIR-coupler chemistry can still run" ([couplers.py L135-147](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/model/couplers.py#L135-L147)).
- `spektrafilm_lut_creator` bakes `.cube`, `.3dl` and Hald bundles in one to four LUT topologies, 33³ by default, with a QA suite (ΔE2000, ΔITP, monotonicity, smoothness) ([README.md L139-143](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/README.md#L139-L143), [builders.py L244-262](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm_lut_creator/builders.py#L244-L262), [qa/metrics.py](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm_lut_creator/qa/metrics.py)). The README lists a LUT bridge in ART as a sanctioned integration ([README.md L22](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/README.md#L22)).

So within its domain a LUT is spektrafilm's own output at grid points, and the only error is interpolation. That error is largest for saturated colours near the spectral locus, where the spectral upsampling and gamut compression bend fastest; the QA suite measures exactly this, and 65³ in a half-float WebGL2 3D texture (about 2 MB per stock) is cheap.

**One gap: scene-linear inputs.** The creator registers Rec.2020 Linear but silences it as a bundle input, because "a uniform [0, 1] .cube domain can't represent scene-linear highlights past diffuse white without a log shaper", which the bundle pipeline does not emit yet ([color_spaces.py L268-296](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm_lut_creator/color_spaces.py#L268-L296)). So we would not use its `.cube` writer; we would run the runtime (`SimulationPipeline.process` with `lut_mode`) over our own grid with a per-channel log2 shaper, and apply the same shaper in GLSL. That is the documented boundary of the runtime ([lut_creator README L51-67](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm_lut_creator/README.md#L51-L67)), not a change to its maths.

**Exposure range.** The profiles' density curves are defined over log10 exposure -3 to +4 relative to grey (256 points; checked in `kodak_portra_800.json`), i.e. about -10 to +13 stops; vkdt clamps to ±4 log10. Emulsion's RAWs clip at 1.8 (about +3.3 stops over a 0.18 grey) before Exposure and the tone equalizer move them. A shaper over about -14 to +10 stops around 0.184 covers the film's whole curve with room for Exposure, with values below the floor clamped as vkdt does (5e-4). The creator's own default for scene-linear inputs puts encoded 1.0 at +6 stops over grey ([color_spaces.py L28-34](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm_lut_creator/color_spaces.py#L28-L34)), which is too little for us.

**What is lost:**

| Effect | Spatial or random? | Our substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Halation and in-emulsion scatter | Spatial | Our halation (Optics), same place in the chain, default 0. Scatter (a 2-10 µm softening) has none. |
| DIR-coupler diffusion (20 µm Gaussian plus 200 µm tail, weight 0.06 main / 0.03 dev; [params_schema.py L139-141](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/runtime/params_schema.py#L139-L141)) | Spatial: the inhibitor correction is blurred before it is subtracted ([couplers.py L95-108](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/src/spektrafilm/model/couplers.py#L95-L108)) | None. It adds edge contrast and local saturation; at 35 mm and 6000 px, 20 µm is about 3 px. Clarity and Sharpening are the nearest. |
| Grain on density | Random and spatial | Our darktable grain on print lightness, default 0. |
| Glare | Random | None (vkdt defaults it to 0 anyway). |
| Scanner unsharp mask, lens blurs, diffusion filters | Spatial | Our Scanner sharpness and Sharpening. |
| Auto exposure, highlight boost | Image-global | Our Exposure and Highlights. |

**What needs re-baking:** anything inside the LUT: film and paper choice, print exposure, enlarger filter shifts, film and paper gamma, coupler amount, preflash, base density scale, scan illuminant, and the output gamut compression settings. Nothing upstream (white balance, Exposure, colour balance, tone equalizer) or downstream needs it. Emulsion exposes none of the in-LUT parameters today, so the only ones that would force multiple bakes are a push mapping (four LUTs per stock) and, if wanted, print-exposure steps for Whites.

## 5. How faithful vkdt is to current spektrafilm

- vkdt follows spektrafilm's **dev** branch, not a release: its bake script reads `presets/couplers.toml` and `grain.toml`, Langmuir coupler coefficients and the B&W profiles `kodak_doublex`, `kodak_trix` and `kodak_2302` ([mklut-profiles.py L11-37, L282-323](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/mklut-profiles.py#L11-L37)), all of which exist only on dev ([dev commit 74f8629](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/commit/74f8629) "kodak trix profile and initial bw support", [2ea9724](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/commit/2ea9724) "dir-couplers langmuir saturation", [8cbfb01](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/commit/8cbfb01) "grain presets"). There is no pinned spektrafilm commit in the module; the readme tells you to clone spektrafilm's default branch ([readme.md L122-135](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/readme.md#L122-L135)).
- The module was restructured into a multi-pass spectral pipeline in July 2026 ([65d6305e](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/commit/65d6305e)), and the 2026 history includes "import upstream licence + b/w stock" ([d3ebadc0](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/commit/d3ebadc0)), "direct port of spektrafilm grain" ([8a1777f8](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/commit/8a1777f8)), "switch film illuminant adaptation from cat16 to spectral" ([62274ec3](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/commit/62274ec3)), "default couplers to 0.5" ([af78a500](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/commit/af78a500)) and "lower scatter default" ([bcc29536](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/commit/bcc29536)).
- Its own words: "best effort ... with some changes for efficiency (for instance the grain model was swapped out), as well as ui and parameters (e.g. scale of enlarger filters). if you find gross differences between spektrafilm and vkdt please let us know, this is probably not intentional" ([readme.md L252-259](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/readme.md#L252-L259)).
- Known differences from what I read: coupler amount 0.5 against spektrafilm's 1.0; scatter 0.2 against 1.0; glare 0 against 3%; neutral print filters from vkdt's own fit (`wb.h`, [readme.md L147-150](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/readme.md#L147-L150)) rather than spektrafilm's `neutral_print_filters.json`; spectral scene and film illuminant controls that spektrafilm lacks; DIR couplers applied as a precomputed inverse curve warp; no output gamut compression; enlarger lamp modelled as a blackbody through KG3 ([setup.glsl L291-322](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/1ded4fb513f32f5281abfaf9ccb1d31c76817b09/src/pipe/modules/filmsim/setup.glsl#L291-L322)).

So vkdt is a good guide to making spektrafilm fast on a GPU, but its defaults are its own. A port that must "stay true to upstream" should take spektrafilm's numbers, not vkdt's.

## Options, most to least contained

### A. Offline-baked 3D LUT per stock (recommended)

- **What changes:** one new stage file (`film.glsl`: per-channel log2 shaper, trilinear 3D texture lookup, sRGB transfer function) used in place of Emulsion, Tone and Print when the stock has a LUT; its TypeScript reference (the same shaper and lookup, tested against points sampled from spektrafilm in Python); a 3D texture per stock loaded once; the Clarity and Sharpening taps read after it; the stock's balance and matrix set to identity. A Python bake script in `tools/` that runs spektrafilm at a pinned commit in `lut_mode` with output BT.2020 linear and its default output gamut compression, and writes the LUT files plus licence and changelog. Python 3.13 is needed only to bake ([README.md L219-221](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm/blob/3bb2c2d2801ff68b92019cf1dbcbb133d60832bc/README.md#L219-L221)). Neutral, every other stage, and the Rust core are untouched.
- **Fidelity:** spektrafilm's own numbers at grid points, colour path complete (spectral exposure, masking and non-spatial DIR couplers, film and paper curves, neutral enlarger filters, spectral scan, gamut compression). Spatial and random effects are dropped, as upstream itself does for LUTs. Interpolation error measurable with spektrafilm's QA metrics.
- **Effort:** small. A few days including the probe check and licensing files.
- **Limits:** in-LUT parameters are fixed; push or print exposure need one LUT per step.

### B. Per-pixel GLSL port of the colour path only

- **What changes:** the same position in the pipeline, but the stage computes spektrafilm live like vkdt: spectral upsampling texture, profile data texture, per-frame setup in TypeScript (illuminants, filters, normalisations, DIR-coupler inverse warp), 36- and 41-band integrals per pixel in GLSL, and a TypeScript reference of all of it. Couplers without diffusion keep it per-pixel.
- **Fidelity:** as good as A if it follows spektrafilm (not vkdt's defaults), with no grid error, and film gamma, print exposure, filters and coupler amount become live controls. It must track a fast-moving upstream by hand.
- **Effort:** large, weeks. WebGL2 fragment shaders lack vkdt's compute shared memory and subgroups, so setup moves to the CPU.

### C. Full negative-print-scan pipeline with spatial effects

- **What changes:** B plus DIR-coupler diffusion passes (a blur in µm between expose and develop), spektrafilm halation and scatter (replacing our Optics halation), density-domain grain (replacing darktable grain), glare, and scanner sharpening. This reorders and replaces existing ported stages.
- **Fidelity:** closest to spektrafilm's full renders.
- **Effort:** largest; and it is **not contained**: it changes halation, grain and sharpening for every stock, which fails Oli's condition.

## Recommendation

Take option A. It meets the "contained" condition literally: one stage swapped in for film stocks, nothing else moved. It is also the most faithful option per hour of work, because the numbers are produced by spektrafilm's own runtime in the mode its author designed for LUT sampling, rather than by our port of it. Use spektrafilm, not vkdt, as the reference: vkdt is a careful GPU port, but it follows an unreleased branch and has its own defaults and fits. If live film and paper controls are wanted later, option B can replace the LUT behind the same stage without moving anything else.
