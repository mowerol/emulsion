Spectral Film stocks, Film grain, new Glow filters, a new Blacks control, better black and white looks, and filename search.

- Spectral Film, a new look collection: 22 film stocks rendered from scene light by darktable's spektrafilm, including Portra 160, 400 and 800 (with Push 1 and Push 2), Gold 200, Ektar 100, Pro 400H, Superia X-TRA 400 and UltraMax 400 on their papers; Vision3 cinema films printed on Vision 2383; Ektachrome, Kodachrome, Provia and Velvia slides; and Double-X and Tri-X in black and white. Two new Optics controls work under every look: Film edges (the edge contrast and colour separation of film development) and Film softness (light scattering in the film's layers). Picking a Spectral Film film sets both to 100. (#72)
- Film grain: a new grain type that rebuilds the photo from film grains and prints them, calibrated to real lab scans, with subtle colour that Amount controls along with the grain. Amount 50 is typical film at the chosen speed. Black and white looks and fully desaturated edits get monochrome grain automatically. The previous grain is now called Standard. (#71, #75)
- Glow has a Glow filter menu in Optics: Black Pro-Mist (as before), Glimmerglass, Pro-Mist and CineBloom. (#73)
- Blacks now uses RawTherapee's Exposure Black curve: deeper blacks that round off into detail, or open near-black detail while true black stays black, without shifting colours. The slider runs −100 to +100. Photos you have edited keep their rendering. (#69)
- Black and white looks convert through darktable's grey mixer, like a colour filter on the lens, and the nine Fuji black and white looks are rematched to the camera's JPEGs. Photos you have edited keep their rendering. (#70)
- Search photos by filename from the search icon in the Library bar and Develop's Images pane. (#68)
- Scrolling a large folder in Import no longer stops the interface. Thanks to MrMota25. (#67)

Known: Blacks has no effect on Spectral Film films yet (#74). Remove can still produce smudges or invented details, especially on difficult backgrounds. The Fuji recipe editor currently supports colour recipes. Recipe detail settings use Emulsion's own grain, clarity and sharpening; a recipe's camera dynamic-range setting, noise reduction and exposure compensation are not applied. Some colours, particularly saturated greens, can still differ from the camera's JPEG. Very heavy exports of GFX100RF photos can still run out of memory.

Please report any problems at github.com/mowerol/emulsion/issues, with the log file attached (Help › Show Log in Finder).
